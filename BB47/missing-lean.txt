Minimal subshifts and uniformly recurrent languages are no longer missing either: WP7 (2026-09-14)
wrote `ForMathlib/Dynamics/SymbolicDynamics/Minimal.lean`, 442 lines, 25 declarations, std3.
Mathlib had NEITHER -- `Dynamics/Minimal.lean` defines a minimal ACTION but has no minimal-subsystem
theorem, and `UniformlyRecurrent` appears nowhere in it.  `Subshift.exists_isMinimal_le`: every
non-empty subshift over a finite alphabet contains a minimal one (Zorn on the bundled lattice in the
order dual, non-emptiness of the chain's `sInf` by Cantor's intersection theorem, which is the only
place finiteness of the alphabet is used).  `isMinimal_iff_isUniformlyRecurrent`: minimal iff every
orbit is dense iff every legal word occurs in every point iff the language is uniformly recurrent,
in both the word form and the bounded-gap form, which are proved equal so that the definition is not
a judgement call.  `isMinimal_iff_addAction`: this IS Mathlib's `AddAction.IsMinimal Z Y`.
BUT NOT what the plan expected it to be for.  Writing it showed that [M3, Lem. 3.4]'s own proof uses
no minimality -- it builds the left-infinite right-special spine, takes limit points and runs a gcd
argument -- and that under (H) Omega(u) need NOT be minimal, since the skew branch of [M3, Thm. 4.1]
has Omega = Orb(x) union P with P a periodic orbit.  What the file serves is the step AFTER
Lem. 3.4 (Thm. 4.1(a) closes with "every point has dense orbit, thus Omega is minimal", which is
`isMinimal_iff_forall_orbitClosure_eq` verbatim) and the vocabulary in which the Coven-Hedlund
classification is stated.  Lem. 3.4's second statement, incidentally, never needed WP7: for
aperiodic x in Omega, `orbitClosure x` is infinite, so p(n, orbitClosure x) >= n+1 = p(n, Omega) >=
p(n, orbitClosure x), and `Subshift.ext_language` finishes -- WP2 + WP4 only.  It stays unwritten
because it is a BB47-side statement under (H).

Theorem Z is no longer missing (2026-09-14): `BB47/TheoremZ.lean`, 372 lines, 23 declarations,

either.

Correction (2026-09-15, out of writing `BB47/M5.tex`): the paragraph above says Proposition C
needs M3 Cor. 4.2 (balance) and through it the Theorem-B chain.  IT DOES NOT.  The base-2 case
follows from p_inf(2)=3 and aperiodicity alone: exactly one 2-block is non-recurrent, and it
cannot be 01 or 10, because after the last occurrence of 01 no 0 may be followed by a 1, which
makes the tail 1^a 0^omega or 1^omega -- eventually periodic, excluded.  The base-b>=3 case is
p_inf(1)=2.  So Proposition C(1)-(2) is formalizable today, and it is the natural next BB47
target after `TheoremZ.lean`: it needs only `pInf`, the horizon and `IsEventuallyPeriodic`, all
of which exist.  Proposition C(3) (the nested self-similar sets) needs M3 Lem. 3.2 (the shape of
G_n under (H)) plus first-return decomposition of walks -- the graph side of that is in
`ForMathlib/Dynamics/SymbolicDynamics/Rauzy.lean`, the IFS side is not in the corpus at all.

Also out of M5.tex: the two-base rigidity theorem (M5 Thm. 5.4) rests on one elementary lemma,
dim_H closure(O_b xi) <= upper box dim <= h(xi,b)/log b, proved by covering the orbit closure with
the p(n,xi,b) closed b-adic intervals of rank n.  ForMathlib HAS box dimension
(`ForMathlib/Topology/MetricSpace/BoxDimension.lean`, with `Metric.dimH_le_upperBoxDim`), so that lemma is
formalizable; what would have to be a cited axiom is the Shmerkin-Wu/Chang-Li-Wu statement that
the exceptional set is a countable union of sets of upper box dimension zero.  Furstenberg's
dimension formula is NOT needed -- only the inequality half, which is the lemma itself.
