\chapter{What Mathlib and ForMathlib supply}\label{app:mathlib}

The Lean files of \texttt{CarryGraph/} import Mathlib and three ForMathlib modules, nothing
else. This appendix lists what is taken from them, what had to be written because it is missing,
and what was deliberately left out. The point of the list is the second column. A reader who
wants to know how much of the book is new mathematics, and how much is bookkeeping around
existing libraries, can read it off here.

\section*{Written here because it is missing}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \emph{Labelled digraphs with finite out-neighbourhoods on an arbitrary vertex type.}
  Mathlib's \lean{Digraph} and \lean{SimpleGraph} carry no labels, and \lean{Quiver} has no
  finiteness. The structure \lean{CarryGraph V A} is one field, \(\succc : V\to A\to
  \mathrm{Finset}\,V\).
\item \emph{Decidable reachability.} Mathlib decides no closure of a relation. Here the
  function \lean{reachSet} iterates successor sets to a fixpoint within \(|V|\) rounds (see
  \lean{mem\_reachSet}), which makes \(\mathrm{Reach}\), the core and the descendant count
  computable.
\item \emph{K\H{o}nig's lemma for finitely branching graphs}, plain and geodesic. Mathlib's
  \texttt{Order/KonigLemma} needs a strongly atomic order or an inverse system, and either wrapper
  is longer than the direct pigeonhole proof (25 lines).
\item \emph{Strong components, ranks, cores, escape depth} (Chapters~\ref{ch:reach}--\ref{ch:core}), \emph{entropy of a
  graph} and its eigenvector brackets (Chapter~\ref{ch:entropy}), \emph{splittings, homomorphisms, simulations,
  subdivision and label products} (Chapter~\ref{ch:splitting}), \emph{covers} and the transfer theorems
  (Chapter~\ref{ch:covers}), \emph{circulations} and the balance lemma (Chapter~\ref{ch:flows}).
\item \emph{A shift lemma for} \lean{expGrowthSup} (\lean{expGrowthSup\_shift}): the growth rate
  of \(n\mapsto u_{n+1}\) is that of \(u\).
\end{itemize}

\section*{Left out}

Perron--Frobenius theory is not formalised (decision D-4 of the plan). The entropy is bracketed
by super- and sub-eigenvectors (Theorem~\ref{thm:brackets}), which is what every
certificate in the corpus uses, and no bridge needs the Perron eigenvalue itself. Likewise the
identity \(h(X)=\max_C h(X_C)\) over strong components \cite[Thm.~4.4.4]{LM95} and the period
of a strong component are mentioned in Chapter~\ref{ch:entropy} but not proved in Lean, and
Tarjan's algorithm is not needed, because strong components are decided through
\lean{reachSet}. None of them is used later.

\section*{Checking a certificate with the kernel}

A theorem about a concrete graph is proved by evaluating a decision procedure in the kernel
(\texttt{decide +kernel}). How the witness is phrased decides whether this is feasible. On the
path graph \(0\to1\to\dots\to n-1\circlearrowleft\) with rank \(\rho(v)=n-1-v\), measured on one
machine with three threads and the time to load the imports subtracted:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrr@{}}
\toprule
form of the check & \(n\) & time & peak memory\\
\midrule
\lean{IsRank} unfolded, vertex pairs and triples & 20 & 0.5\,s & 2.4\,GB\\
 & 40 & 4.5\,s & 3.7\,GB\\
 & 80 & \multicolumn{2}{r}{killed at 12\,GB}\\
arc-local, \lean{Finset}-valued successors & 100 & 2\,s & 3.5\,GB\\
 & 300 & 26\,s & 9.7\,GB\\
 & 1000 & \multicolumn{2}{r}{killed at 12\,GB}\\
arc-local \lean{Bool} check, \lean{List}-valued successors & 1000 & 0.4\,s & 1.6\,GB\\
 & 10\,000 & 2.5\,s & 2.3\,GB\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The defining form quantifies over all pairs and triples of vertices and costs \(|V|^3\). The
arc-local form costs one pass over the arcs, but kernel reduction of \lean{Finset} membership
through \lean{Multiset} quotients is slow. The practical rule, which the large certificates of
the corpus already follow, is therefore: state the check as a \lean{Bool} function on lists,
prove once that it implies \lean{IsRank} (or \lean{IsLRank}), and let the kernel evaluate only
the \lean{Bool} function. \texttt{decide} on the defining predicates is for examples with a few
dozen vertices.
