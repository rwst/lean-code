## What is reachable from the dubC1 plan's toolkit

**1. Confinement certificates — the mod-1 twin of the unavoidable-set method, and it already exists.** Flatto–Lagarias–Pollington prove lim sup − lim inf ≥ 1/p for {ξ(p/q)ⁿ} using exactly this species of machinery: decoupled "linear mod one" maps, subshift analysis, and — the key point — their property (C) can be verified for *specific intervals by finite computation* (they show Z₃/₂(s, s+1/3) = ∅ for s = 0, 1/6, 1/3, 1/2, 2/3) . This is a "core is empty" certificate on the interval-partition side, structurally identical to the plan's pruning engine. A genuine new-results pipeline: run the X2′ ladder on confinement subshifts to enlarge the known emptiness atlas — current frontier: a union of three intervals of total length 19/39 always contains a limit point of {ξ(3/2)ⁿ}, and no interval of length < 1/2 is known to always contain one . The barrier at the end of this road is precisely Mahler's Z-number problem (the cell [0, 1/2)), still open, with no Z-numbers below 2⁵⁷ computationally . Same architecture as the plan: a ladder of finite certificates, one named conjecture as the ceiling.

**2. Parity for all ξ splits into a finite check plus a named barrier.** "[ξ(3/2)ⁿ] even i.o. for every ξ" ⟺ "every realizable carry word hits {δ ∈ {0, 2}} i.o." (since xₙ ≡ δₙ mod 2). The plan's automaton method can test the SFT over-approximation of the carry-word shift directly. It will fail — odd-forever words (δ ∈ {−1, 1}) correspond to orbits confined in explicit cells of [0,1), so the failure witnesses are exactly a confinement set, and killing it is a Z-type emptiness problem of the same genus as route 1. So parity-for-all-ξ is not a different problem from the mod-1 range problem; it's a special case with the same barrier shape. (I found no published proof even of the ξ = 1 case "even i.o." in the standard references — treat that as open pending a gate check.)

**3. The metric line (T4's twin) is unclaimed territory in the same way.** Almost-every-ξ equidistribution is known (Koksma), but the *structure of exceptional sets* is where the transfer-operator/dimension machinery applies: Dubickas and Akiyama construct ξ with the closure of {ξ(p/q)ⁿ} of Hausdorff dimension ≤ log q/log(p/q) when p ≥ q² + 1 . Dimension formulas, decay tables, and confinement-set dimensions for the p < q² regime — the same "computable, decreasing sequence" deliverable as T4, on the mod-1 side, with the same tools.

**4. One thing to borrow for the b = 7 paper itself.** The 2026 negative-rationals paper proves its range bound via a complexity/Morse–Hedlund argument on non-eventually-periodic integer sequences, with DN Lemma 2 as the non-periodicity input  — that is the same "aperiodicity is the barrier" dichotomy as the plan's two regimes, transplanted to the fractional side. It's the right citation for positioning the plan's Regime 2 against the mod-1 literature.

## Verdict

From the b=3, c=2 instance of strategy K there is **no path to parity or equidistribution through the certificate/composites machinery** — not because of a gap in the plan, but because those questions live on the solenoid coordinates (carry word, fractional orbit) that the mod-M residue graphs quotient away; for ξ = 1 parity is literally a binary digit of 3ⁿ. What the plan's *toolkit* (subshift, pruning, entropy, finite certificates) can reach is the FLP/Dubickas confinement line: range bounds, cell-emptiness certificates, dimension of exceptional sets — with Mahler's Z-number problem and Furstenberg-type rigidity as the two honest ceilings, to be stated T6-style rather than attacked.
